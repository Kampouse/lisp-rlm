Abstract. Pre-constructed Publicly Verifiable Secret Sharing (PPVSS)
extends conventional Publicly Verifiable Secret Sharing (PVSS) by requiring the dealer to publish a commitment or encryption of the shared
secret, enabling more efficient and versatile constructions for a variety of
cryptographic protocols. In this paper, we further enhance this paradigm
by introducing Packed Pre-constructed PVSS (3PVSS), which allows a
dealer to encode multiple secrets within a single polynomial while maintaining the pre-constructability property. We present two constructions
of 3PVSS schemes. In the first construction, the dealer publishes a single
commitment to all shared secrets, providing a compact representation
that is particularly suitable for applications requiring efficient communication. In the second construction, the dealer publishes individual commitments to each secret, enabling greater flexibility in applications that
require independent verification of multiple shared values. Both schemes
preserve the key advantages of PPVSS, including the optimistic reconstruction approach enabled by pre-constructability. We demonstrate that
these two variants naturally support different applications. Using the first
3PVSS construction, we revisit the ALBATROSS randomness generation protocol proposed by Cascudo and David (ASIACRYPT 2020) and
present a more efficient variant with reduced computation and communication costs. As an application of the second construction, we extend
the universally verifiable e-voting protocol recently proposed by Baghery,
Knapen, Nicolas, and Rahimi (ACNS 2025) to support multi-candidate
elections, while preserving universal verifiability. Our results show that
the resulting protocols outperform their original counterparts in terms
of efficiency.
Keywords: Publicly Verifiable Secret Sharing · Pre-Constructed PVSS
· E-voting · Randomness Generation · Packed Secret Sharing.

Introduction

Secret sharing is a fundamental cryptographic technique that allows an entity,
called the dealer to securely distribute a secret s among a set of n parties, such

2

Karim Baghery, Eleftheria Makri, and Dheeraj Kumar Suryakari

that only authorized subsets of these n parties can reconstruct the secret. Unauthorized subsets of the parties learn nothing about the secret. Shamir secret
sharing [24] is one of the first secret sharing schemes (appeared concurrently
with Blakley’s scheme [6] in 1979), and one of the most widely used constructions. Secret sharing serves as a core building block in numerous cryptographic
protocols designed to eliminate reliance on a single trusted entity, including applications in multiparty computation and threshold cryptography.
Classical secret sharing schemes (e.g., Shamir secret sharing [24]) on their
own offer passive security. This means that privacy is guaranteed against an adversary who passively corrupts any subset of parties smaller than the authorized
subsets. In this context, passively refers to trying to learn as much information
as possible about the secret, without deviating from the protocol specification.
As such, attacks against the integrity of the shares are not considered. A malicious dealer/party is allowed to deviate from the protocol specification in an
arbitrary manner, for instance by distributing/providing incorrect values during
the secret sharing/reconstruction phase. Such adversaries can be thwarted by
Verifiable Secret Sharing (VSS) schemes [1, 3, 4, 7, 13, 15, 22, 25]. VSS allows the
parties to verify the consistency of the shares provided by the dealer/parties during the sharing/reconstruction phase, and if a corrupted dealer/party attempts
to provide inconsistent shares, this behavior is detected. Publicly Verifiable Secret Sharing (PVSS) [4, 8–10, 18–21, 23] goes a step further than VSS, allowing
anyone, including external observers who do not participate in the protocol, to
verify that the shared values are consistent. This is particularly useful in application scenarios where public auditability and minimal interaction are necessary,
such as e-voting systems, auctions, privacy-preserving finance, distributed randomness generation, and privacy-preserving federated learning.
The notion of Pre-constructed PVSS, where the dealer is required to additionally commit to the shared (reconstructed) secret, was recently introduced
by Baghery, Knapen, Nicolas, and Rahimi [5]. As observed in [5], this notion
not only improves the efficiency of existing PVSS-based applications, but also
offers greater versatility than standard PVSS. Baghery et al. [5] further show
that the PVSS by Schoenmakers [23], as well as its pairing-based variant by Heidarvand and Villar [20] are a special case of Pre-constructed PVSS. However,
their cost, notably the quadratic in the number of parties cost of Schoenmakers’
PPVSS, in terms of exponentiations required during verification, gives rise to
new, more efficient schemes. Several follow-up works [4, 8, 9, 11] have improved
the efficiency of the original PVSS by Schoenmakers [23], albeit at the cost of
losing the re-constructability property.
Packed secret sharing schemes [15,17] improve the efficiency of Shamir secret
sharing by encoding ℓ secrets into one single polynomial. This can be achieved,
without increasing the share size. However, given that for a t-degree polynomial
at most t + 1 secrets can be securely encoded, to achieve packing of ℓ secrets the
degree of the polynomial should be increased to t+ℓ and the corruption threshold
should be decreased such that n ≥ 2t + ℓ in the synchronous communication
model. As a result, an inherent tradeoff between privacy and robustness arises in

Packed Pre-Constructed PVSS for Randomness Generation and E-Voting

packed schemes, concretely offering t-privacy and (t+ℓ)-reconstruction. Building
on this idea, packed variants of VSS and PVSS [2, 9] extend their standard
counterparts to the packed setting. In these schemes, a dealer can distribute
multiple secrets simultaneously while allowing parties, or even the public in the
case of PVSS, to verify the correctness of the distributed or broadcasted shares.
This significantly improves the amortized efficiency of PVSS and VSS protocols
when multiple secrets need to be shared.

Packed Pre-Constructed PVSS

For Guaranteed Output Delivery (GOD), several distributed applications rely on
techniques based on VSS. For example, the randomness beacon ALBATROSS [9]
employs PVSS schemes. These secret sharing schemes allow a dealer to prove
the validity of encrypted shares to anyone using ZK proofs, without revealing
any information about the secret or the shares themselves. However, when the
dealer later reveals the secret, verifying its correctness typically requires simulating the secret-sharing phase of the (P)VSS protocol, which is inefficient. This
inefficiency arises because the dealer initially publishes only encrypted shares,
and the accompanying ZK proof guarantees consistency of the shares with some
secret, without revealing any information about the secret itself. In 2025, PreConstructed PVSS (PPVSS) was introduced in [5]. This primitive extends PVSS
with additional functionality and provides a novel and significantly more efficient
approach for secret reconstruction when the dealer is available. This setting is
common in distributed protocols built on VSS, such as DKG protocols and randomness beacons. In essence, the new reconstruction approach enables much
more efficient verification of the revealed secret.
The goal of this section is to extend the notion of PPVSS to the packed
setting, which we refer to as Packed Pre-constructed PVSS (3PVSS). Conceptually, 3PVSS can be viewed as a PPVSS construction built on top of packed
secret sharing schemes. While packed secret sharing is known to sacrifice some
robustness, it has been shown in [9] that packing can nevertheless be leveraged to
design more efficient randomness beacon protocols. In this paper, we show that

3PVSS not only improves the efficiency of such applications in many settings,
but can also enable new applications.
3.1

Definitions

We begin by naturally extending the definition of PPVSS from [5] to the packed
setting. In this variant, the dealer publishes commitments to the shared secrets
and publicly proves the well-formedness of the commitment(s) together with the
encrypted shares. At the level of the definition, we do not specify the particular
commitment mechanism used by the dealer. Later in this section, we present
two concrete 3PVSS constructions: in the first, the dealer commits to all packed
secrets using a single group element, while in the second the dealer publishes
individual commitments for each secret.
Definition 2 (3PVSS). A 3PVSS scheme should have four algorithms, namely,
Initial, Share, Verify and Reconstruction whose descriptions are as follows:
- Initial (1λ ) → ({P Ki , SKi }ni=1 , h0 ): When given 1λ , each party Pi for 1 ≤

In 1999, Schoenmakers [23] proposed a universally verifiable binary e-voting
protocol based on (P)PVSS. In his construction, voters use a PPVSS scheme
to submit encrypted votes together with proofs of validity, while a set of tallying authorities jointly reconstructs the final outcome. In this protocol, the
verification phase requires O(mn2 ) exponentiations, where n and m denote the
number of talliers and voters, respectively. Recently, Baghery et al. [5] revisited Schoenmakers’ protocol and improved its efficiency by introducing a more
efficient PPVSS construction. They also observed that this application inherently requires a PPVSS scheme. Their approach reduces the verification cost to

Packed Pre-Constructed PVSS for Randomness Generation and E-Voting

O(mn) exponentiations, significantly improving scalability for elections with a
large number of talliers.
However, both the original protocol [23] and its improved version [5] are designed for binary elections, where voters must choose between two candidates.
Schoenmakers outlined a generic approach for extending such protocols to the
ℓ-candidate setting by running the binary protocol ℓ times. Unfortunately, this
approach increases both computation and communication costs by a factor of ℓ.
In particular, for the current state-of-the-art protocol [5], the voters’ computation and communication costs become O(nℓ) exponentiations and O(nℓ) group
elements, respectively, while the ballot verification cost grows to O(mnℓ) exponentiations. In large-scale elections with many talliers and candidates, these
costs can become impractical. In this section, we show how our proposed 3PVSS
protocol Λindv
RO can be used to efficiently extend the binary e-voting protocol
to the ℓ-candidate setting. Compared to Schoenmakers’ generic approach, our
construction significantly reduces the computational and communication overhead. In particular, the voters’ computation and communication costs decrease

A Randomness Beacon alternative to ALBATROSS

Randomness Beacon is required in applications like e-voting and anonymous
messaging to provide fresh unbiased random values to all the parties. In 2020,

Packed Pre-Constructed PVSS for Randomness Generation and E-Voting

23

Cascudo and David published ALBATROSS [9], the state-of-the art randomness
beacon protocol based on Packed PVSS. In ALBATROSS each party reveals their
secrets as part of the randomness beacon protocol, but to prove that the revealed
secrets are valid they have to reveal the whole secret polynomial itself. Thus, if
someone wants to verify the secrets’ validity then they have to simulate the whole
sharing phase of the underlying PVSS protocol, this is a costly computation
procedure incurring a cost of quadratic in the number of parties.
In this Section, we present our randomness beacon protocol in Figures 8
and 9. We also show that in many cases our construction is efficient than ALBATROSS. To put simply, we replace the building block being Packed PVSS
with our 3PVSS Λsingle
RO , which additionally gives a single commitment that represents all secret random values of the dealer. In Subsection 5.1, we discuss a
bottleneck in ALBATROSS protocol, followed by our alternative construction
for ALBATROSS. By the end of this section, we not only show that our protocol outperforms ALBATROSS in many cases but also acknowledge the case
where we are not computationally efficient. The main highlight we show is that
in terms of dealer communication, we outperform ALBATROSS in every case.
5.1

On the Bottleneck of ALBATROSS

The main bottleneck of ALBATROSS is in its Reveal phase (description in Figure
8 in Section 4.3 of [9]). More precisely, the goal of the Reveal phase is to reveal
the secrets of whom the dealer has done the secret sharing. But in order to verify
that those values are actual secrets, in ALBATROSS, a verifier has to simulate
the whole sharing phase of the dealer with the values that were revealed. This is
because, the underlying proof system of a general PVSS says that if the PVSS
verification algorithm outputs true, then the encryptions of the secret shares
correspond to some unique secret with high probability.
Whereas, in the case of (P)PPVSS, if the verification algorithm outputs true
then it means that the encryptions of the secret shares correspond to the unique
secret(s) which was already committed by the dealer with high probability. So, the
intuition is that if one uses a Pre-Constructed PVSS, that can commit to multiple
secrets in a single polynomial, over the standard PVSS used in ALBATROSS (a
Random Beacon protocol) then we show that the efficiency can be improved.

RO .

Conclusion

We introduced Packed Pre-constructed Publicly Verifiable Secret Sharing (3PVSS),
a new primitive that combines the benefits of packed secret sharing with the preconstructability property of PPVSS. Our construction enables a dealer to distribute multiple secrets simultaneously while committing to the reconstructed
values, thereby preserving the optimistic reconstruction approach and public
verifiability that make PPVSS attractive for practical distributed protocols.
We presented two practical 3PVSS constructions with complementary properties. The first construction, Λindv
RO , publishes individual commitments to each
secret, offering greater flexibility and enabling applications that require independent verification of shared values. The second construction, Λsingle
RO , publishes a
single commitment to all secrets, resulting in improved communication efficiency.
Together, these constructions demonstrate that the 3PVSS paradigm can support different design goals depending on the requirements of the application.
To illustrate the usefulness of our approach, we applied the two constructions to two important distributed protocols. Using Λsingle
RO , we revisited the
ALBATROSS randomness beacon protocol and obtained a more efficient variant with reduced computational and communication costs. Using Λindv
RO , we extended a recently proposed universally verifiable e-voting protocol to support
multi-candidate elections while maintaining efficiency and public verifiability.
Overall, our results show that extending PPVSS to the packed setting provides a powerful tool for designing more efficient distributed cryptographic protocols. We believe that the 3PVSS paradigm can serve as a useful building block
in a variety of applications beyond those considered in this work, including distributed key generation, threshold cryptography, and secure multi-party computation. Exploring further applications of 3PVSS and extending our constructions
to other PPVSS frameworks remain interesting directions for future research.

In this section, we reduce the security of modified-PDL (given in Def. 3) to the
standard DL assumption. We present the reduction for the representative case
t = 1, ℓ = 2, and n ≥ 4, where the hidden polynomial has degree at most 2.
The reduction embeds the DL challenge as one polynomial evaluation and then
simulates the entire modified-PDL transcript for the adversary. In particular,
the remaining group elements, including the aggregate term F0 , are generated by
choosing sufficiently many polynomial evaluations at random and interpolating
the rest in the exponent.
